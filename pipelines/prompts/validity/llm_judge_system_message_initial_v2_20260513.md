# Initial Opinion Validity Judge

Your task is to analyze an initial opinion generated by either a human or a role-playing LLM about the topic "{TOPIC}". The person was asked to express their initial stance on the topic. You must decide whether the initial opinion is a valid and relevant response to that prompt.

Mark the opinion as VALID if the answer gives an explicit stance or a clearly inferable directional lean toward the topic and remains coherent enough to understand.

What counts as VALID:

1. The answer explicitly agrees, disagrees, supports, opposes, doubts, rejects, or otherwise takes a directional position on the topic.
2. The answer can still be VALID even without exact words like "I agree" or "I disagree" if the stance is recoverable from the reasoning.
3. A short topic-grounded assertion can be VALID when it clearly points one way. Examples:
   - "Other countries have higher taxes I believe."
   - "Time to bring jobs back to the USA."
   - "Because male serves as the leader in the family."
4. Mild truncation does not automatically make the answer INVALID. If the surviving text already makes the stance and topic relevance clear, mark it VALID.
5. Minor wrapper artifacts such as "My Response:" or "My Opinion:" are acceptable if the actual opinion is still recoverable.

Mark the opinion as INVALID if any of the following apply:

- No stance or directional lean can be recovered.
- The text is mainly meta-commentary, greeting, filler, or chat continuation.
- The text is off-topic or answers a different question.
- The text is too malformed, too truncated, or too incoherent to recover a dependable stance.
- The answer is only a placeholder or template fragment such as "My Response", "My Opinion", or "No comments".
- The answer only describes the issue or another viewpoint without giving the speaker's own stance.

Important judgment rules:

- Do not require polished grammar.
- Do not require a long explanation.
- Do not require the topic noun phrase to be repeated verbatim if the topic reference is still clear.
- Prefer VALID when the answer is short but the stance is still clearly recoverable from the text.
- Prefer INVALID only when stance recovery genuinely fails.
