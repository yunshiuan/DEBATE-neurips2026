# Post Opinion Validity Judge

Your task is to analyze a post-conversation opinion generated by either a human or a role-playing LLM. After discussing the topic "{TOPIC}", the person was asked to state whether they agree or disagree with the topic and explain why. You must decide whether the post-conversation opinion is a valid answer to that prompt.

Mark the opinion as VALID if the speaker's final stance on the topic is explicit or clearly inferable from the answer itself and the text is coherent enough to understand.

What counts as VALID:

1. Short final stances are VALID when they clearly express the speaker's end position on the topic. Examples include answers like:
   - "I agree."
   - "Agree"
   - "Certainly disagree"
   - "100% agree"
   - "Yes" or "No" when the prompt polarity makes the stance obvious.
2. Brief but directional topic summaries are VALID if they clearly support or reject the claim.
3. The answer does not need to restate the full topic noun phrase if references like "this", "it", or "the statement" clearly point to the discussion claim.
4. A response can still be VALID when it is concise, somewhat informal, or lightly truncated, as long as the final stance is still recoverable.
5. Discussion-summary answers are VALID if they still reveal the speaker's own final stance, for example by saying that the speaker and others agreed with the topic or that the speaker still disagrees.
6. Topic-grounded criticism or praise can imply stance even without explicit "I agree" wording if the direction is obvious from the answer.

Mark the opinion as INVALID if any of the following apply:

- No final stance can be recovered from the answer itself.
- The text is mainly meta-commentary about the discussion, such as "learned a lot", "very insightful", or "it was interesting".
- The answer only says the speaker still believes their original stance without restating what that stance is.
- The answer is off-topic, filler, greeting, placeholder text, or unrelated chat.
- The text is too malformed or too truncated to recover a dependable final stance.
- The answer only describes the topic or another view without making the speaker's own position recoverable.

Important judgment rules:

- Do not over-penalize brevity.
- Do not require a long justification when the final stance is already clear.
- Prefer VALID for short but clearly directional end-position answers.
- Prefer INVALID only when the final stance or topical grounding genuinely cannot be recovered from the answer itself.
